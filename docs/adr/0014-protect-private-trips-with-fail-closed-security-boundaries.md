# Protect private Trips with fail-closed security boundaries

Flash Trips will treat authenticated Trip content as private personal data and exact travel dates, locations, companions, budgets, and constraints as Sensitive Trip Data. Security authority remains in deterministic application controls: the identity provider authenticates an External Identity, while Flash Trips owns Invitations, application sessions, Planner Access Status, Operator grants, Trip ownership, Approvals, disclosure, deletion, and recovery. Protected work fails closed whenever identity, session, access status, ownership, revocation, or required security-audit state cannot be established.

The initial threat model covers malicious guests, malicious or compromised Planners, compromised Operator credentials, hostile provider and model content, leaked provider secrets, vulnerable dependencies, compromised deployment credentials, and ordinary external network attacks. A compromised Planner device, a malicious cloud administrator, and a compromised approved provider remain residual risks rather than threats the application claims to eliminate.

## Identity, sessions, and ownership

The browser holds only opaque, high-entropy application-session cookies marked `Secure`, `HttpOnly`, and `SameSite`. State-changing cookie-authenticated requests also require allowed-origin validation and a session-bound CSRF token; safe GET requests never mutate state. Server-side session records allow immediate revocation by session, Planner, or External Identity. Session identifiers rotate at authentication, privilege changes, recovery, and periodically. Planner sessions expire after twelve hours of inactivity or seven days absolutely, with no initial persistent “remember me” option.

Every Trip-scoped command, query, SSE connection, Approval, Run action, handbook retrieval, export, and deletion re-establishes active Planner status and server-side ownership. Opaque resource identifiers are references rather than authority, and foreign, unauthorized, and nonexistent resources are indistinguishable. Client-supplied Planner identifiers, email, roles, ownership, actors, and timestamps carry no authority. Authorization uses principal-scoped application repositories, relational ownership constraints, transactional commit-time rechecks, and exhaustive cross-Planner tests. PostgreSQL Row-Level Security is not initially required because background Run execution has system authority and an unproven transaction-local principal design could create a false safety boundary.

Recent authentication is required for identity linking or unlinking, all-session revocation, Planner closure or recovery, bulk personal-data export or deletion, Operator bootstrap, and every Operator mutation. Operator and Planner grants use distinct External Identities and sessions. Operator endpoints never accept Planner sessions, and Operator authority never grants access to Trip content.

Application suspension blocks protected operations immediately. External-provider account disablement takes effect no later than token refresh, with a maximum one-hour grace period; a reported compromise therefore triggers application suspension instead of waiting for identity-provider propagation. A compromised Planner response revokes sessions, cancels mutating Runs, supersedes pending Approval Requests, blocks handbook access, and starts Operator-assisted recovery to a verified replacement External Identity. Historical records remain immutable, but an Access Recovery Review of security-sensitive actions since the last known-safe time must complete before mutations, Approvals, and handbook access resume. Disputed committed changes use Plan Amendments rather than rollback.

If the Operator identity is compromised or unavailable, an audited offline recovery operation controlled by the deployment owner freezes Operator mutations, revokes the old grant and sessions, verifies a replacement exact External Identity, and binds it once. There is no permanent universal administrator or emergency Trip access.

## Data minimization and privacy

The pilot refuses passport and government identifiers, payment data, loyalty credentials, exact home addresses, full birth dates, immigration status, diagnoses, prescriptions, vaccination records, emergency contacts, and free-form medical or legal documents. A Companion has no account. The Planner may provide an optional label, travel-relevant age category, and narrowly typed Companion Travel Constraints without legal names or explanations of medical, religious, or other private circumstances. Planners must be adults and confirm they are authorized to provide any Companion details; the product is not directed to children.

The pilot collects no marketing profile or campaign consent and uses no third-party behavioral analytics, advertising pixels, session replay, or nonessential tracking cookies. Before collecting Trip data, Flash Trips discloses data categories and purposes, Sensitive Trip Data risks, provider and model processing, retention, security limitations, Planner controls, Companion responsibility, offline handbook limitations, and contacts for privacy and security requests. A production identity, model, travel, telemetry, email, or hosting provider must have reviewed purposes, data categories, regions, subprocessors, training restrictions, retention and deletion terms, breach process, transport security, and access controls. Unknown terms fail closed.

Sensitive Trip Data uses enforced transport encryption and managed database and backup encryption at rest. Provider and application secrets live in Key Vault. Per-field application encryption is deferred because the initial architecture establishes no separate database-operator trust boundary and the added key and query complexity would not remove endpoint compromise risk. Recoverable credentials are never stored in application data; token-like values use protected hashes or keyed digests.

Guest Session, Guest General, and Guest Intake Transfer are deferred from the invitation-only release, which requires authentication before all Trip Intake. If later enabled, Guest Sessions remain anonymous, browser-bound, and restricted to general cited travel answers. Their cookies rotate at authentication and are consumed once by an atomic, idempotent Guest Intake Transfer. Authentication never automatically claims a transcript: the Planner reviews and explicitly confirms a typed summary into a new Trip Request. IP addresses and user agents may be soft abuse signals but never identity or ownership evidence.

## Untrusted content and external calls

The initial product accepts bounded text and structured actions but no uploaded files and no arbitrary user-directed URL fetches. Deterministic routing authorizes a closed set of read-only tools, providers, destinations, and typed parameter bounds. Models may propose schema-constrained values but cannot create credentials, headers, network destinations, citations, actionable identifiers, permissions, or workflow transitions.

Planner, provider, search, and model text remains untrusted data. Prompts separate it from task instructions, use only minimum category-specific context, enforce length limits, and require structured outputs. Provider content passes source-specific schemas, provenance, encoding and markup normalization, URL checks, field bounds, and deterministic rejection reasons. Reasoning calls receive only task-required dates, locations, budgets, and typed constraints, never identities, contact details, labels, unrelated Trip fields, or wholesale conversation history.

Outbound source retrieval uses HTTPS, revalidates DNS and every redirect, and blocks private, loopback, link-local, metadata, credential-bearing, and nonstandard destinations. It carries no browser cookies, JavaScript, or ambient credentials and enforces time, byte, redirect, content-type, nesting, candidate, token, and output limits. Exceeding a bound produces a typed limitation or failure; truncation cannot silently alter authoritative meaning.

Planner-visible model and provider prose renders only as escaped plain text or a tightly sanitized Markdown subset with raw HTML disabled. Every link is validated independently, executable and remote embedded content is prohibited, and the browser uses a restrictive Content Security Policy.

## Abuse controls

Rate limits are versioned configuration classes. If the deferred Guest surface is introduced, Guest Sessions allow a burst of three Turns per minute, ten per hour, and twenty per day; each IP allows thirty per hour and one hundred per day, while external model or search calls are limited to ten per Guest Session per day. Planners initially allow 120 reads per minute, thirty commands per minute, ten new Runs per hour, and thirty new Runs per day. SSE allows three concurrent connections per Run and ten per Planner with authenticated cursors, heartbeats, bounded idle time, and stale-connection replacement.

Ordinary guest and request counters may remain in-process only while exactly one replica operates; their restart limitation is accepted. Invitation acceptance, identity recovery, Operator mutations, deletion requests, and repeated ownership violations use durable counters. Exact idempotent HTTP retries count against request rate but do not consume another command, Run, provider, model, or monetary allowance. Same-key, different-content conflicts are suspicious mutations.

Limits return `429` with `Retry-After` without disclosing the triggering dimension. Ordinary excess progresses through throttling, recent authentication, session revocation, and Operator alert rather than automatic permanent suspension. Repeated cross-Planner access attempts, recovery abuse, or clear token replay may trigger a Protective Access Hold; only an Operator controls permanent suspension or closure. Forwarded client addresses are trusted only from the configured ingress chain. Raw network identifiers remain restricted diagnostics for seven days, while durable audit records use protected digests.

Invitation acceptance uses a random single-use secret retained only as a protected digest plus an authenticated External Identity whose verified email exactly matches the Invitation. Possession of the link is insufficient. Attempts are non-enumerating and durably rate-limited; Operators may revoke or reissue an Invitation but cannot recover its original secret.

## Least privilege, secrets, and supply chain

Production and test have separate resources and managed identities. Next.js, FastAPI, deployment, migration, and any future worker identities receive only required permissions. Database runtime, migration, backup and restore, and diagnostic roles are separate; runtime cannot create schemas, alter roles, disable constraints, or manage backups. Applications retrieve individually authorized Key Vault secrets through managed identity with version-aware bounded in-memory caching.

Pull-request workflows receive no production secrets, deployment credentials, or live provider/model access. Protected post-merge or manually approved deployment workflows use environment-scoped GitHub OIDC. Production Trip data is never copied to test, development, CI, or evaluation; those environments use synthetic fixtures. Any future production-derived diagnostic data requires an explicitly approved irreversible de-identification process.

All secrets support versioned rotation and immediate replacement on suspected exposure. Recoverable provider and client secrets default to ninety-day rotation and application cryptographic keys to 180 days; managed identities and OIDC replace long-lived credentials where possible. Protected records include a non-secret key version. Old verification keys remain narrowly accessible only until dependent records expire. Compromise marks affected integrity claims untrusted and triggers scoped review.

A leaked provider credential deactivates the adapter and new calls, revokes and rotates the secret, and audits observed use. Fallback occurs only through an already-qualified equivalent provider. Key exposure derives `Revalidation Required` only when Evidence integrity may have been affected. A deployment compromise freezes promotion, revokes federation, verifies image digests and audit history, rotates reachable runtime secrets, and redeploys a known-good immutable digest before authority is restored.

Runtime egress is logically restricted to activated identity, model, provider, and validated-source paths; infrastructure network enforcement remains an Azure-topology decision. Deployment additionally requires locked dependencies, GitHub Actions pinned by commit SHA, dependency and secret scanning, static analysis, an SBOM, container vulnerability scanning, immutable image digests, and build provenance. Critical or high findings block release unless a documented compensating control, owner, and expiry are approved.

## Audit, retention, and deletion

Security Audit Records are separate from execution Inspection Records and Application Insights. They cover Invitations, sessions, identity links, Planner access changes, Operator actions and recovery, authorization denials, Guest Intake Transfer, Access Recovery Review, suspension effects, handbook and bulk-data delivery, deletion, secret rotation, and deployment Security Incidents. Ordinary authorized Trip reads are not security events.

Audit records contain only event kind, opaque actor, subject and resource references, role, result and reason, server time, correlation, producer version, and protected session or network digests when needed. They exclude Trip content, destinations, prompts, provider payloads, secrets, and raw network identifiers. Security-sensitive mutations and their audit record commit atomically or fail closed. Audit records are append-only, sequence-checked, and corrected through supersession.

Planners may see their own recent sessions, identity changes, exports, deliveries, and recovery events. Operators see Invitation, access-status, recovery, secret, and deployment events without Trip content. Developers use synthetic records; production access is through the Operator security surface. No Operator or developer break-glass access to private Trip content exists initially.

If the deferred Guest surface is introduced, Guest content is purged within twenty-four hours after session expiry or transfer and minimized guest abuse data lasts thirty days. Expired and revoked sessions and terminal Invitation personal data are removed within thirty days. Trips are automatically deleted 24 months after the later of travel completion and meaningful Planner activity, with advance warning, unless the Planner explicitly deletes the Trip sooner. Deletion blocks access immediately and purges live data within thirty days. Planner closure blocks access, allows verified recovery for thirty days, then deletes Planner-owned data; an authenticated explicit deletion may waive the grace period.

Raw provider/model payloads are not persisted by default and any required encrypted temporary payload is removed within twenty-four hours after the Turn or Run terminates. Execution Checkpoints expire within seven days after terminal outcome, temporary Run artefacts within thirty days, and production Replay Bundles within thirty days when lawful. Minimized Inspection Records and canonical Evidence follow the Trip lifecycle. Provider or privacy restrictions may shorten every period and make replay unavailable.

Minimized Security Audit Records last one year, Application Insights telemetry thirty days, and raw network-bearing security diagnostics seven days. Deleting a Planner removes identifiable audit links and retains only a non-identifying Deletion Receipt. Backups remain encrypted and unavailable to normal reads, expire no later than thirty-five days, and are not selectively rewritten; any restore reapplies deletion and revocation ledgers before service resumes. There is no informal Operator legal-hold feature.

Planners receive recent-authenticated, idempotent operations for personal-data export, Trip deletion, and Planner closure/deletion. Exports include application-owned profile and identity-link metadata, relevant Invitations, retained Turns, Trips and revisions, Approvals, Evidence attribution, Runs and safe inspection views, handbooks, deliveries, and Planner-visible security events. They exclude secrets, internal defenses, other identities, restricted provider payloads, and Operator-only records. Deletion overrides ordinary immutability and removes identifiable Plan Revisions, Evidence, handbooks, Inspection Records, and audits; already downloaded files cannot be recalled.

## Incident response and assurance

Critical Security Incidents include suspected cross-Planner disclosure, authorization bypass, active credential misuse, and deployment compromise. High incidents include contained secret exposure and repeated targeted abuse; isolated denied attempts and policy violations are routine. Automated containment does not wait for human acknowledgment.

Independent kill switches disable Guest access, new sessions, Invitations, Operator mutations, model calls, each provider adapter, new Runs, canonical commits, handbook delivery, and bulk export. Missing control configuration fails closed. When confidentiality or authorization integrity is uncertain, critical safe mode exposes only deterministic service-status and recovery information; narrower incidents disable only the affected boundary.

Alerts cover cross-Planner denials, Protective Access Holds, Operator recovery, secret exposure or unusual Key Vault access, audit-write failure, deployment drift, repeated Invitation or recovery abuse, deletion failure, and critical dependency findings. Incident evidence is bounded, access-restricted, owned, and expiring. Affected Planners receive prompt factual communication and protective guidance according to applicable legal and provider requirements.

Environment recovery rebuilds from a known-good immutable image, rotates reachable secrets, invalidates sessions, verifies infrastructure and deployment identities, reapplies deletion and revocation ledgers, validates audit continuity, and passes security smoke tests. Actively exploitable critical vulnerabilities are disabled or patched immediately; other critical findings target forty-eight hours, high seven days, medium thirty days, and low planned maintenance. Exceptions require a compensating control, owner, expiry, and audit.

Registration and CI require authentication edge cases, a complete principal-by-use-case authorization matrix, adversarial prompt and provider fixtures, SSRF and browser-injection cases, resource bounds, privacy and secret canaries across every output channel, retention/deletion recovery, and abuse controls. Before invitation production, Flash Trips must also pass session and identity revocation, Operator recovery, secret and key rotation, adapter kill-switch, critical-safe-mode, known-good deployment, backup-with-deletion-replay, and alert-delivery drills.

The invitation release additionally requires the accepted identity spike, reviewed threat model and privacy notice, approved provider terms, no unresolved critical or high findings, independent security review, a verified backup restore, and a monitored vulnerability/privacy contact. Security review repeats before material identity, input, provider, sharing, replica, or infrastructure-boundary changes and at least every ninety days during the pilot.

The accepted residual risks are the one-hour identity-provider disablement grace, restartable ordinary in-process limits, absence of PostgreSQL RLS and application field encryption, one deployment owner for offline Operator recovery, no high-availability claim, inability to recall downloaded handbooks, and compromise risk on Planner devices, cloud administrators, and approved providers. Flash Trips describes implemented controls rather than claiming generic security, anonymity, end-to-end encryption, certification, or legal compliance.
